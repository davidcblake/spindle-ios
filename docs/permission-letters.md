# Permission letters (drafts for Dave)

Two short letters. Neither is needed to keep Spindle running for friends on
TestFlight; they are what makes the talk checking "10/10" before the App
Store: written permission for the list of talks Spindle keeps, and a second,
scholarly source to check references against.

Fill in the bracketed parts, and send from your own address.

---

## 1. Intellectual Property Office, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

**To:** Intellectual Property Office — through the permission request linked
from the Terms of Use page on churchofjesuschrist.org (check the page for the
current form or address; I haven't confirmed it)

**Subject:** Permission request — a free scripture study app linking to general conference talks

Dear Brothers and Sisters,

I am [Dave Blake], a member of the [ward/stake] and currently serving as
second counselor in our stake presidency. I have built a small, free iPhone
app called Spindle to help members study the scriptures. A person chooses a
passage, and Spindle prepares a short, Christ-centered study guide with
questions to ponder and an invitation to act. It has no advertising, does not
sell anything, and collects no personal information beyond what the person
chooses to save on their own phone.

Study guides sometimes point to a general conference talk that teaches the
same principle. To make sure every talk named is a real one, and to send the
reader to the talk itself in Gospel Library, Spindle keeps a list of
conference talks since 1971 with only four facts for each: the speaker, the
title, the conference, and the talk's address on churchofjesuschrist.org. It
keeps no talk text, quotes none, and uses no Church logos or artwork. The list
is refreshed twice a year, after each general conference, by reading the
conference index pages once, slowly.

I would be grateful for:

1. Permission to keep and use that list of talk facts and links in the app; and
2. Any guidance on how you would like Spindle to describe its relationship to
   the Church. It currently says it is an independent app, not made or
   endorsed by the Church, and that everything it prepares should be read
   alongside the scriptures and the words of living prophets.

If any part of this is not appropriate, I will gladly change it. Thank you for
your service and for considering this request.

Respectfully,
[Dave Blake]
[Phone] · [Email]
[Ward and stake]

## 2. BYU Scripture Citation Index

**To:** The Scripture Citation Index team, Brigham Young University
(scriptures.byu.edu — contact link on the site)

**Subject:** Using the Scripture Citation Index in a free scripture study app

Dear Scripture Citation Index team,

Thank you for the Scripture Citation Index. I use it often.

I have built Spindle, a free iPhone app that prepares short, Christ-centered
study guides for a passage a person chooses. Each guide may point to two
general conference talks that teach from that passage. Today Spindle checks
each talk against a list of real conference talks so it never names one that
does not exist. The Citation Index would let it go one step further: to
prefer talks that actually cite the passage being studied.

Would you be open to Spindle using the Index for that purpose — for example,
through a data export, an API, or an agreed, light use of the public site?
Spindle would show only the talk's title, speaker and conference, link to the
talk in Gospel Library, and credit the Scripture Citation Index by name. It is
free, without advertising, and makes no money.

I would welcome any conditions you would like, and I'm happy to talk by phone.

With appreciation,
[Dave Blake]
[Phone] · [Email]
